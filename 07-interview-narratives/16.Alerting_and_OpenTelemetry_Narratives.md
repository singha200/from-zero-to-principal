# OpenTelemetry & Alerting Narratives

## 1. Tell me about a time you fixed "Alert Fatigue" on your team.
**Context**: When I joined the Platform team, the on-call engineer was receiving over 200 alerts a week. Most were ignored or auto-resolved. The "boy who cried wolf" effect was active; when a real SEV-1 happened, it was missed in the noise, causing a 2-hour outage.

**Action (The Purge)**:
- I instituted "Alert Bankruptcy." I declared that any alert without a runbook attached was to be immediately disabled. 
- Analyzed the highest volume alerts via PagerDuty metrics. Found that 60% of alerts were noise (e.g., "Worker Pod CPU Spiked above 85% for 1 minute").
- **The Shift**: I migrated our Prometheus rules from *Cause-based* to *Symptom-based* SLI alerting. We stopped alerting on CPU and Memory. We *started* alerting exclusively on Error Rates and Latency Percentiles (P99 > 500ms).
- **The Result**: Page volume dropped from 200/week to 5/week. Crucially, every single page was now actionable, and engineers stopped hating their on-call rotations.

## 2. How did you standardize observability across a fragmented microservice architecture?
**Narrative**: Shows the ability to influence cross-functional teams and avoid vendor lock-in.

**Context**: Our company had 50 Polyglot microservices (Java, Go, Node.js). Some teams used Datadog, some used New Relic, others just logged to console. When an incident spanned multiple services, debugging was impossible because distributed traces broke across vendor boundaries.

**Action**:
- I couldn't force everyone onto one vendor overnight, but I could force them onto one semantic standard: **OpenTelemetry (OTel)**.
- I set up an **OTel Collector DaemonSet** on our Kubernetes clusters.
- I wrote paved-road base libraries for our top 3 languages that automatically injected OTel SDK auto-instrumentation (meaning developers didn't have to manually write tracing spans).
- I used the OTel Collectors to emit the exact same data to multiple backends simultaneously. The Java team could stick with New Relic temporarily, and the new Node team could use Grafana/Tempo. Because the telemetry format (OTLP) was identical, we finally achieved cross-service Distributed Tracing. 

## 3. Describe your strategy for managing dashboards.
**Narrative**: Dashboards aren't just pretty pictures; they are triage tools.

**Action**:
- My philosophy is "Grafana-as-Code." I don't let engineers build raw dashboards in production because they eventually drift or get accidentally deleted.
- I built standard JSON dashboard templates using **Jsonnet**. Every microservice got a free "Golden Signals" dashboard automatically generated and deployed to Grafana via CI/CD pipelines. This dashboard prominently displayed Request Rate, Error Rate, and P99 Latency at the very top.
- During a SEV-2, an Incident Commander didn't have to figure out how Team A's bespoke dashboard worked versus Team B's. Every team's dashboard followed the exact same cognitive layout, dramatically reducing Mean Time To Resolution (MTTR).
