# CI/CD, GitOps & OpenShift Pipelines Narratives

Interview questions at the Staff/Principal level regarding CI/CD focus on migrating legacy massive systems, standardizing developer-experience, and solving the bottlenecks of Cloud-Native tooling.

## 1. Tell me about a time you modernized a legacy CI/CD architecture.
**Context**: We had a colossal monolith of a Jenkins Master managing 300+ microservices. The Groovy scripts were thousands of lines long. The Master frequently ran out of heap memory, bringing all company deployments to a halt.

**Action**: 
- **The Strategy**: I did not try to rewrite 300 pipelines overnight. I established a strangler-fig pattern.
- **The Tech**: I introduced OpenShift Pipelines (Tekton).
- I built a standard "Golden Path" pipeline utilizing decoupled, reusable Tekton Tasks (git-clone, buildah, sonarqube, argocd-sync).
- **The Execution**: I identified just 3 low-risk microservices and migrated them as a proof-of-concept. I utilized Pipelines-as-Code so developers could trigger builds simply by opening GitHub PRs.
- To drive adoption, I didn't force migrations. Instead, I highlighted that the new Tekton pipeline completed in 4 minutes (compared to Jenkins' 12 minutes due to executor queue times). Word spread, and teams *wanted* to migrate. Within 6 months, we deprecated Jenkins for 90% of our stateless applications.

## 2. How did you handle ArgoCD performance issues when scaling to thousands of apps?
**Narrative**: Highlighting a deep understanding of Kubernetes control planes and ArgoCD's internal architecture.

**Context**: As our multi-cluster Hub & Spoke ArgoCD architecture grew past 2,000 Applications, developers complained that hitting "Sync" in ArgoCD took 5+ minutes to reflect in the cluster.

**Action & Triage**:
- I analyzed the ArgoCD `application-controller` metrics. I found the work queue depth was consistently overflowing. 
- ArgoCD defaults to 20 threads. We increased it to 50 threads, which helped briefly, but then the `repo-server` (which runs `helm template`) began CPU throttling.
- **The Permanent Fix**: 
  1. I implemented **Controller Sharding**. I spun up 5 replicas of the `application-controller`, each responsible for a subset of the OpenShift clusters.
  2. I disabled the default 3-minute git-polling mechanism and switched entirely to **GitHub Webhooks**. This prevented ArgoCD from needlessly scanning 2,000 repositories, instantly dropping CPU usage on the `repo-server` by 70%.

## 3. How do you prevent bad code from reaching Production in a highly automated GitOps pipeline?
**Narrative**: "Moving fast" is useless if you're deploying bugs. Staff engineers build "Guardrails."

**Action**:
- **Continuous Integration (The Left Side)**: In the Tekton PR pipeline, I integrated strict gates. If SonarQube detects < 80% test coverage, or Snyk detects a High CVE, the Tekton pipeline fails, blocking the ability to merge the PR.
- **Continuous Deployment (The Right Side)**: Even if bad code merges, ArgoCD deploys it using **Progressive Delivery (Argo Rollouts)**.
- **The Magic (Automated Rollbacks)**: Argo Rollouts shifts 10% of traffic to the new Pods. A Background Analysis job queries our Prometheus monitoring. If the new Pods show a > 1% HTTP 500 error rate over a 5-minute window, the rollout is automatically aborted, and traffic shifts 100% back to the old, stable version. The developers can then debug the issue on their own time without any customer impact.

## 4. What is the biggest danger of using Kubernetes-native CI/CD like Tekton?
**Narrative**: Pointing out the hidden tradeoffs of "shiny new tech" shows maturity.

**Answer**: Data bloat and Etcd destruction. Jenkins keeps memory on disk; Tekton keeps memory in Kubernetes Secrets, ConfigMaps, and Custom Resources. Every PipelineRun creates 10+ K8s objects. At scale (thousands of builds a day), if you don't aggressively prune `TaskRuns` and `PipelineRuns`, your cluster's Etcd database will surpass its 8GB limit and permanently crash the OpenShift cluster. Understanding this forced me to implement strict cron-based pruning before we ever went live with Tekton.
