# Staff Engineering & Leadership QNA

Moving from Senior to Staff involves transitioning from "how to build" to "what to build and why." You are expected to operate with high autonomy, influence without authority, and manage cross-functional complex systems.

## 1. How do you handle technical disagreements across teams?
**Narrative**: At a Staff level, disagreements are rarely about an objective "right" answer; they are about differing constraints (e.g., speed vs. reliability).
**Action**:
- **Listen first**: Understand their key metrics and constraints. Is the other team penalized for latency, cost, or delivery speed?
- **Write an RFC/ADR (Architecture Decision Record)**: Formalize the problem, propose 2-3 options, and clearly state tradeoffs.
- **Align on Data**: Use data to break ties (e.g., load test results, actual cost projections).
- **Escalate safely**: If consensus cannot be reached, escalate to leadership with the ADR highlighting the unresolvable constraint. "If we do X, we hit our quarterly goal but incur technical debt. If we do Y, we delay by a month but scale better." Let the business decide the tradeoff.

## 2. How do you prioritize Technical Debt vs. Feature Work?
**Narrative**: Business leaders care about revenue, not clean code. To sell tech debt cleanup, I translate it into business value.
**Action**:
- I categorize tech debt into: 
  - **Dangerous** (causes outages)
  - **Taxing** (slows down developers)
  - **Aesthetic** (ugly code but works).
- I tie "Dangerous" debt to our SLA/error budgets. If we breach error budgets, feature work freezes until reliability is restored (SRE culture).
- I tie "Taxing" debt to developer velocity ($ spent vs output).
- I suggest a 20% continuous engineering allocation for tech debt within every sprint.

## 3. Tell me about a time you drove a cross-team initiative.
**Narrative**: Focus on *influence without authority*.
**Action**:
- Describe a project (e.g., migrating 50 microservices to Kubernetes).
- **Discovery**: Interviewed engineering managers to understand their pain points with the current VM-based deployment.
- **Prototyping**: Built a paved-road CI/CD pipeline and migrated ONE low-risk service to show success (faster deploy times, less manual intervention).
- **Advocacy**: Presented the success story in the Engineering All-Hands.
- **Scaling**: Created self-serve migration tooling and documentation so teams could migrate themselves. Gamified the process by tracking migration status on a centralized dashboard.

## 4. How do you balance "Building it Right" vs "Building it Fast"?
**Narrative**: Perfection is the enemy of shipping, but shipping broken software destroys trust.
**Action**:
- **Identify the blast radius**: If a feature is a completely new experiment, build it fast. If it's the core payment gateway, build it right.
- Use **Feature Flags** and **Canary Deployments** to decouple deployment from release. This allows us to build fast, test in production with real traffic (but isolated impact), and iterate carefully.
- "We don't compromise on security or observability, but we can compromise on scalability for V1 and refactor later."

## 5. How do you approach Mentorship and uplifting the team?
**Narrative**: A Staff Engineer is a multiplier. Output = Your output + (N x Mentored Engineers' Output).
**Action**:
- Code reviews: I don't just point out mistakes; I point to documentation patterns or best practices.
- Pair programming on complex architectural tasks.
- Running internal "Tech Talks" or "Incident Reviews" (blameless post-mortems).
- Reviewing RFCs/Design Docs from mid-level engineers to guide their architectural thinking *before* they code.
